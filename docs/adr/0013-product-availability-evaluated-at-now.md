# A Produit's availability is evaluated against *now*, never against the chosen Créneau

Some Produits are only served during part of the day — a Menu Midi exists at 12h30 and not at 20h. A Produit therefore carries an **Horaire de disponibilité**, a weekly pattern of hours (day of week, `HH:MM` start, `HH:MM` end, local to `Europe/Paris`) that decides whether it is on the Carte, and a Produit outside it is **Hors carte**: absent from the Carte, refused at add-to-cart and at payment, its own page still reachable and showing the hours where the button used to be.

Two instants could decide this, and they never coincide: **the instant the customer is looking**, and **the Créneau de retrait they will collect in**. The Créneau is chosen at checkout, after the cart is filled. **We evaluate against the present instant.**

## Why *now* and not the Créneau

The Créneau is the more truthful answer — it is at 12h15 that the kitchen plates the Menu Midi, not at 11h05 when the customer is reading — and it is unusable, because **the Carte does not know any Créneau**. The customer does not have one until checkout. A Carte that wanted to respect Créneau semantics would have to show every Produit available for *at least one* of the day's remaining Créneaux, which leaves the Menu Midi on the Carte at 20h — the exact thing this decision exists to prevent — or it would have to ask for the Créneau **before** the Carte, inverting the whole ordering flow for one feature. Evaluating against *now* is computable on every surface from one piece of data, and it is what the Mode vitrine already does on the same page.

The cost is real and is paid in configuration, not in code. At 11h05 a customer who will collect at 12h15 does not see the Menu Midi, and at 13h55 one who will collect at 14h20 can still buy it. Both are corrected by moving the hours — open at 11h00, close at 13h45 — and the Délai de préparation already bounds how far a Créneau can drift from the moment of ordering. What must not be done is to correct them with a second rule: a Produit whose availability depended on *both* instants would be Hors carte for reasons no admin screen could display.

The consequence to state plainly, because it looks like a bug and is not: **a Menu Midi ordered at 13h55 for a 14h20 Créneau is a valid order.** It was bought during the service. The kitchen has it in hand.

## Why the Carte cannot learn this from `/store/products`

The obvious implementation — filter the Hors carte Produits out of the Store product list — does not work here, and fails in the direction that looks fine in development.

The storefront reads products with `cache: "force-cache"` and a tag from `getCacheOptions("products")` (`apps/storefront/src/lib/data/products.ts`). That cache is invalidated by product writes and by nothing else: **it will never expire at 14h00 on its own.** A filter applied inside `/store/products` would keep serving the Menu Midi until somebody happens to edit a product in the admin, possibly days later. On a dev server, where the cache is cold on every reload, it would appear to work perfectly.

So the state is served by a **dedicated Store route**, `GET /store/product-availability`, read with `next: { revalidate: 60 }` — the shape `retrieveShowcase` already uses for the Mode vitrine, for the same reason and on the same page. It answers, for each Produit that has at least one Horaire de disponibilité (a handful, never the Carte), its `product_id`, its schedule, and a server-derived `available_now`. A Produit absent from the response has no Horaire and is on the Carte permanently.

ADR 0010 refused to let the Carte read `orders_open` precisely because that flag "can never be cached at all", and accepted the Mode vitrine because sixty seconds of staleness cost nothing there. Hors carte falls on the Mode vitrine's side of that line, not `orders_open`'s: it flips a few times a day and stays flipped for hours, so a minute of lag leaves a card on screen slightly too long and never lets anything through — the add-to-cart hook refuses from 14h00 sharp regardless of what the page is showing.

The derivation itself is a **pure function shared with the validation hooks**, on the precedent of `getOfferableSlots`, whose own comment gives the reason: both callers need "what is offerable right now", and must derive it the same way "or the two can silently drift apart".

**Rejected: extending `/store/showcase`.** It is already fetched by the Carte page, so it would cost zero extra round trips against the one-per-minute the separate route costs. Refused because the Mode vitrine is *decided* and suspends the whole shop, while Hors carte is *derived* and concerns one Produit — CONTEXT.md keeps those apart on purpose, and a single payload carrying both would make that distinction look like an accident of implementation.

**Rejected: shipping only the schedules and evaluating `now` in the storefront.** It is tempting — the storefront renders per request (it reads cookies), so its `now` is always fresh and the flip would have no lag at all. Refused because it puts a second implementation of the time-window rule in a codebase that shares no code with the backend and has no test framework, which is the drift `getOfferableSlots` exists to prevent. The payload carries the schedules anyway, for the product page's "Disponible le midi, de 11h30 à 14h00", so this remains reachable later without a contract change.

## Why Hors carte does not propagate into a Formule's Curation

A Produit Hors carte whose Variante is curated into a Formule's Composant **stays choosable there**. At 12h05, a Menu Midi may legitimately be filled with a dish that is not itself on the Carte.

Filtering those Variantes out of the Curation is the intuitive answer and it opens a cascade nobody asked for. A Composant could then reach **zero** allowed choices at certain hours; every Composant is mandatory (spec Formules, v1); so the Formule becomes impossible to fill; so it would have to go Hors carte in turn — a state derived from another derived state, by a transitive rule that makes a Formule vanish because a dish it lists went off at 18h, with no screen able to explain why.

The Curation is already the guard, and it is human by decision: ADR 0001 required dishes to be ticked into a Formule one by one, and CONTEXT.md accepts the resulting chore because "a dish missing from a formule is noticed and fixed in seconds". Curating an evening dish into a lunch Formule is a data-entry mistake of that same family, fixed in that same place. Answering it with a derived rule would take back the control ADR 0001 deliberately gave the restaurateur.

Mitigation, which is where the cost actually belongs: the Curation screen shows each Variante's Horaire de disponibilité beside its name — "Nems du soir *(servi 18h–22h)*" — so the mistake is visible at the moment it is made.

## Consequences

**Availability belongs to the Produit, not the Variante.** A Formule has exactly one Variante (ADR 0001), so the two are identical for today's need. Produit is chosen because it is the granularity of what disappears — a card in the Carte grid is a Produit — and because a Variante-level rule would produce a card rendered with a half-empty or wholly empty Variante selector, forcing a Produit-level rule to be invented on top of it anyway. Curation is Variante-level because it protects **margin**, where two Variantes differ in price; an Horaire protects no margin. "The large soup only in the evening" is therefore inexpressible, and the answer if it ever arrives is two Produits, or reopening this decision — not an extra column.

**A new `availability` module, edited from a widget on the Produit's detail page.** Not the `pickup` module, which carries the retrait and whose glossary this would falsify, and not a global settings page: the spec Formules already drew this line for the Curation — "contrairement aux Horaires de retrait (configuration globale du restaurant), la Curation est une propriété d'une Formule précise" — and an Horaire de disponibilité falls on the same side. The question "when is the Menu Midi served?" is asked where the Menu Midi is. The row carries a plain `product_id` text column, as `Formule.product_id` does; a Module Link is for the genuine many-to-many the Curation needs, not for a row owned by one Produit. The widget shows on **every** Produit, not only Formules — the rule was attached to the Produit, and no Horaire remains the default.

**Two validation hooks, mirroring the Sélection.** `addToCartWorkflow` / `updateLineItemInCartWorkflow` refuse the common non-malicious case — a Carte opened at 13h50 and clicked at 14h05 — with a message naming the Produit and its hours. `completeCartWorkflow.hooks.validate` re-checks before payment authorisation, since the payment page may have been opened before the hour turned. The Hors carte check joins the existing per-line loop **beside the Sélection check**, leaving the hook's order (Mode vitrine → Créneau → per-line) untouched: a doubly invalid cart still reports the Créneau first, which is the behaviour Créneau and Sélection already have between them. Recovery follows the precedent of `feat/reprise-creneau-expire` — remove the offending line and continue, never start the cart again.

**Saisie rules copied from `CreateScheduleSchema`**: `HH:MM` 24-hour local time, `start_time < end_time` (so no window crosses midnight — free, since no Créneau crosses midnight either), overlapping windows allowed and simply unioned, and an `active` boolean per row. Overlaps are *not* refused the way overlapping Périodes d'annonce are, and the asymmetry holds: two overlapping Annonces silently suppress one another and the missing one is undiagnosable from the storefront, whereas two overlapping Horaires produce exactly the union the restaurateur sees.

**No manual "take this off the Carte now" switch.** Medusa's product `status` already removes a Produit from `/store/products` entirely, which is the right answer for "no more duck at 12h40". A second switch beside a native one that already does it means two places to check when a dish fails to appear.

**A Fermeture exceptionnelle has no effect on availability.** It closes the retrait; the Carte stays browsable, Menus Midi included at midday. Nothing is orderable that day anyway, because no Créneau exists. Deriving availability days from the Horaires de retrait was rejected for the same reason ADR 0007 keeps the two closing calendars apart: the day the restaurant opens click & collect on Saturday evening, the Menu Midi would reappear on Saturday evening without anyone having asked.

**The Carte's section nav must be fixed with this feature.** `CarteSection` already returns `null` for an empty section, so a section whose Produits are all Hors carte disappears by itself. `CarteSectionNav`, however, is built from every root category at page level, so it would keep an entry pointing at an anchor that no longer exists in the DOM. That defect predates this feature — an empty category already produces it — but this feature makes it happen every evening on the most visible section, so it is fixed here: the nav keeps only the entries whose section is actually in the DOM, which it can do because it is already a client component observing those anchors. Deriving the nav at page level instead would require loading every section's Produits in the shell, destroying the `<Suspense>` streaming the spec *Commander depuis la page Carte* was built around.

**The Produit's own page stays reachable, and must.** That spec guarantees product pages remain addressable for "liens directs, partage, référencement". Letting Hors carte 404 them would flip `/products/menu-midi` between 200 and 404 several times a day, which is how a URL gets deindexed — a search-ranking cost paid for a display feature. The page renders the hours instead of the add-to-cart button, which also keeps the commercial information ("this restaurant does a 13,90 € lunch") that hiding it entirely would destroy.

**Related surfaces need no work, verified.** The home page's "Explorez notre univers culinaire" is three hard-coded category tiles loading no Produit. "Vous aimerez aussi" renders thumbnails that link to a Produit's page without an add-to-cart, so a Menu Midi appearing there at 20h leads exactly to the page this decision keeps alive and unorderable.
